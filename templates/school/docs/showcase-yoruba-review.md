# Yoruba showcase content: for a fluent speaker to review

`test/fixtures/showcase/yoruba-jss1.json`, `-jss2.json` and `-jss3.json` were
written by a model, not a Yoruba speaker. They pass the structural check but
nobody fluent has read them. Before the showcase goes in front of a school,
a fluent speaker should read these files and correct what is wrong.

Lesson notes, mark-scheme points and the teacher's feedback are in English.
Questions, readings, cards, model answers and learners' answers are in
Yoruba, with short English glosses in brackets.

## Items the writer was least sure of

**JSS1**

- Greetings: "Ẹ kú ìrọ̀lẹ́", "Ẹ kú àbọ̀" against "Ẹ káàbọ̀", "Ẹ kú ilé", "Ó dàárọ̀"
  as a night farewell, "Ẹ pẹ̀lẹ́", "Ẹ má bínú", "Ẹ kú ọ̀nà".
- Courtesy: "Ẹ jẹ́ kí n ràn yín lọ́wọ́"; the dọ̀bálẹ̀/kúnlẹ̀ split by sex.
- Dialogue: "Ọmọ ọdún mẹ́wàá ni mí", "Ó wọ́n jù", "Ẹ dín in kù", "Ẹ gbà á",
  "Ẹ kú ọjà", "kí ló dé tí o fi pẹ́", "Ara mi kò yá", "N kò ní pẹ́ mọ́".
- Proverbs and poetry: the three proverbs and their meanings; the definition of
  rárà (week 4, deliberately vague); "Iṣẹ́ ni oògùn ìṣẹ́".
- Folktale and riddles: "Ìjàpá àti iṣu" ("wa iṣu", "Ẹ mú olè", "alárékérekè");
  riddle answers abẹ́rẹ́ and ejò; "àlọ́ àpamọ̀" = riddle, "ìtàn àlọ́" = folktale.
- Alphabet and grammar terms: fáwẹ́ẹ̀lì àìránmúpè/àránmúpè, ẹ̀ṣẹ̀-ìsàlẹ̀, gbólóhùn
  kúkúrú/àsopọ̀/oníṣọ̀kan, ọ̀rọ̀ àpèjúwe; "Mi ò jẹun" against "Mo kò jẹun"; the
  pronoun table.
- Numbers, days and the twelve months ("oṣù kìíní").

**JSS2**

- Tense and negation: "Mi ò tíì jẹun", "Mi ò ní jẹun", "Màá jẹun",
  "A kò ní ṣeré lọ́la", and the judgement on "Mo ti ń jẹun".
- Pronoun object and possessive forms (mi, ọ, i, wa, yín, wọn).
- Numbers 11 to 20 (mọ́kànlá to mọ́kàndínlógún), ọgbọ̀n, ogójì, àádọ́ta,
  ọgọ́rùn-ún; ordinals kìíní to kẹwàá; the date pattern "Ọjọ́ kẹta oṣù Bélú";
  the month mapping (Ọ̀wàrà = October, Bélú = November, Èrèlé = February);
  "aago méjì àbọ̀".
- Minimal pairs (ìyá/ìyà, ilé/ilẹ̀, ẹgbẹ́/ẹ̀gbẹ́); the passages "Ọjọ́ kan ní
  ọjà Bódìjà" and "Bàbá Tọ́pẹ́ àgbẹ̀"; "inú dídùn", "ń pèsè oúnjẹ", "tì í lẹ́yìn".
- The week 8 model compositions and week 9 vocabulary lists.
- Informal-letter conventions ("Bàbá mi ọ̀wọ́n", "Ọmọ yín", "Ọ̀rẹ́ rẹ",
  "Mo nírètí pé", "N óò dé", "iṣẹ́ àṣetiléwá").

**JSS3**

- Essay linkers: "bákan náà", "yàtọ̀ sí èyí", "gbólóhùn kókó".
- Formal letters: "Olórí ilé ìwé tí mo bọ̀wọ̀", "Ọmọ ilé ìwé yín tí ó ń gbọ́ràn".
- The original poem "Ìyá mi" (akéwì, àtúnsọ, àfiwé) and the drama
  "Òtítọ́ ni ọ̀nà" (jẹ́wọ́, kábàámọ̀, ìtọ́ni ìgbésẹ̀).
- Names and meanings: Ayọ̀, Tọ́pẹ́ ("tó ọpẹ́"), Ọlá, Adé, Ìbùkún; the naming
  ceremony "on the eighth day"; "ìdánimọ̀", "ẹ̀rọ alátagbà", "ayélujára".
- Occasion greetings and replies ("Ọdún á yabọ̀", "Ara á yá", "Ọlọ́run á tù yín
  nínú", "Àṣẹ"); syllable divisions with kọ/kọ́/kọ̀, ìrì/ìrìn, sè/ṣe.
- Kinship and body vocabulary ("bàbá ńlá", "ìyá ńlá", "aya", "Àbúrò bàbá mi").
- Spelling "ìjàm̀bá" (accident).

## Mistakes that are meant to be there

Learners' answers contain real learner errors on purpose. Do not correct these:
"Mo kò", "Wọn ń ṣeré" for "Wọ́n", "mẹ́dógún", "ọ̀rọ̀" with no tone marks,
"Isẹ ni ogun ise", "Ìyà mi" where "Ìyá mi" is meant, "Ibádan ni ilé mi", and
answers in English or pidgin among the weak ones.
